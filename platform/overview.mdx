If you build software other businesses use, you can offer WhatsApp inside your product without any of them dealing with Meta.

Each of your customers gets an isolated LazyApp workspace: their own connection, phone numbers, templates, contacts and data. They integrate only with your product. You integrate only with LazyApp.

## The shape of it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a customer">
    ```bash
    curl https://lazyapp.in/v1/platform/customers \
      -H "Authorization: Bearer $LAZYAPP_API_KEY" \
      -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
      -d '{
        "name": "Acme Retail",
        "external_id": "acct_8812",
        "billing_mode": "postpaid",
        "markup_bps": 1500
      }'
    ```

    `external_id` is your own account id, so you never need a mapping table.
    `markup_bps` is your margin in basis points — 1500 is 15%.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Send them a setup link">
    A one-time URL where they connect their WhatsApp account through Meta's Embedded
    Signup. You never handle their credentials. See [setup links](/platform/setup-links).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Wait for customer.connected">
    Onboarding is asynchronous and can fail on Meta's side. Handle both
    `customer.connected` and `setup_link.failed` [webhooks](/webhooks/events) rather than
    assuming a redirect means success.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Act on their behalf">
    Mint a customer-scoped API key:

    ```bash
    curl https://lazyapp.in/v1/platform/customers/{customer}/api-keys \
      -H "Authorization: Bearer $LAZYAPP_API_KEY" \
      -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
      -d '{"name": "Acme server", "scopes": ["messages:send", "messages:read"]}'
    ```

    That key can only see that customer's workspace. Every other `/v1` endpoint then
    behaves exactly as documented, scoped to them.

    Alternatively, keep one platform key and pass `X-LazyApp-Customer` on each request.
    The key needs `customers:act` (send only) or `customers:write` (full management).
    See [Authentication](/authentication#acting-for-a-platform-customer).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Billing modes

| Mode | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `inherit` | Billed on your account, as your own usage |
| `prepaid` | Draws down a wallet balance. Sending stops at zero |
| `postpaid` | Accrues and is invoiced |

Set `spending_limit_cents` to cap a customer, and `usage_alert_cents` to receive a `customer.usage_alert` webhook before they hit it. Read consumption with [`GET /v1/platform/customers/{customer}/usage`](/api-reference/platform/retrieve-customer-usage).

## Lifecycle

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="suspend / restore" icon="pause">
    Blocks sending immediately, keeps everything. For non-payment or abuse. Reversible.
  </Card>
  <Card title="archive / restore" icon="box-archive">
    Retires a workspace you may want back. Reversible.
  </Card>
  <Card title="delete" icon="trash">
    Erases the workspace. Irreversible — prefer archive.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Embedding UI

Rather than rebuilding an inbox, drop LazyApp components into your product with short-lived, origin-locked tokens. See [embedded components](/platform/embedded-components).
